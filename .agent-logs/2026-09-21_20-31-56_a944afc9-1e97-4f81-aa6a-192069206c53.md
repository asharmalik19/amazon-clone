Short answer: Phase 3 is anchored to the screenshots for *provenance*, not capped by them for *quantity*.

**What the specs actually bind you to**

- `specs/roadmap.md:69` — `seed/products.json` is "transcribed from `amazon_screenshots/`: ~6 categories, ~40–60 products".
- `specs/tech-stack.md:96-101` — product data is "derived from the reference screenshots": real titles, prices, ratings transcribed; **descriptions and key-info bullets are written by us** in the reference style; images are local placeholders, never hotlinked.
- `specs/tech-stack.md:104` — `seed/products.json` is the single source of catalog truth, nothing fetched at runtime.

So the constraint is: don't invent a catalog out of thin air, and don't scrape Amazon live. The screenshots are the sampling frame for titles/prices/ratings. Prose is yours to write.

**Do 12 screenshots limit you to a tiny catalog?** No. The first one alone (`11.00.28 AM.png`) has ~20 distinct product tiles — a books shelf, Kindle Scribe/Colorsoft variants, gaming, a kitchen-appliances category block with Cooker/Coffee/Pots and Pans/Kettles. Across 12 screenshots, 40–60 products and ~6 categories is comfortably reachable; the numbers in Phase 3 are a target for a catalog that feels dense enough to be real, not a ceiling the screenshots impose.

**The one real constraint** is *category mix*, not count. These are captures of a personalised Amazon home page, so the visible inventory skews to whatever was surfaced — books (heavily), e-readers, kitchen, gaming. If you want six clean, evenly-weighted categories, you'll likely be stretching a couple of them thin, or padding with products that appear only as a thumbnail with a truncated title ("Pathfinders: Extraordin…", "Making Things Move DI…"). Truncated titles are fine to complete sensibly — that's the same latitude the tech-stack already grants for descriptions.

Nothing in the roadmap stops you from adding more screenshots, either. If you want a stronger category spread, capturing a few department pages before Phase 3 starts would be a spec-compliant way to widen the frame — and cheaper than doing it after `products.json` exists.
